# Chapter 7: Product Manager

## 7.1 Purpose

The Product Manager is accountable for the value of the product: deciding what is worth building, and judging whether what is delivered creates that value. The Product Manager runs the Value layer and brings the business into Delivery. They decide what and why, never how.

## 7.2 Where the role works

| Layer | Part |
|---|---|
| [Value](../04-value-layer.md) | Runs the layer |
| [Delivery](../03-delivery-layer.md) | Brings the business into the layer: intent, acceptance criteria, validation |
| [AI](../02-ai-layer.md) | No role |

## 7.3 Responsibilities

### 7.3.1 In the Value layer
As defined in [4.7.1](../04-value-layer.md#471-product-manager):
- **Initiatives.** Every initiative states the value it expects on both sides, how success will be recognized, and when it stops.
- **Priorities and roadmap.** What is pursued, kept, and dropped, and how the roadmap reflects it.
- **Commitments.** What is committed to Delivery and what is promised outward, each on its stated basis.
- **Realization.** Delivered change reaches customers as value, and every outward claim stays true.
- **Decisions on evidence.** Stage changes, stopping, retiring, and decisions on reported debt, each recorded with its reasoning.
- **Reporting.** The company knows how the product contributes to the strategy, and peer plans know what they can rely on.

### 7.3.2 In the Delivery layer
As defined in [3.7.2](../03-delivery-layer.md#372-product-manager):
- **Intent quality.** Every intent states what is wanted and why. Questions about the intent are answered by the Product Manager.
- **Acceptance criteria.** The Product Manager confirms that the acceptance criteria capture the intent before delivery starts.
- **Business half of feature knowledge.** Intents and the business why.
- **Validation.** Validation results are written with the Agentic Engineer, with one named owner.

## 7.4 Behaviors run

- **Value:** all seven behaviors: [Discover](../04-value-layer.md#451-discover), [Shape](../04-value-layer.md#452-shape), [Prioritize](../04-value-layer.md#453-prioritize), [Commit](../04-value-layer.md#454-commit), [Realize](../04-value-layer.md#455-realize), [Measure](../04-value-layer.md#456-measure), and [Steer](../04-value-layer.md#457-steer).
- **Delivery:** [Explain](../03-delivery-layer.md#351-explain), shared with the Agentic Engineer, and [Validate](../03-delivery-layer.md#354-validate), together with the Agentic Engineer under one named owner.

In both layers the AI is an instrument. Every draft it produces becomes knowledge or a decision only when the Product Manager confirms it. How each behavior is set up is defined in [6.5](../06-implementation-guidelines.md#65-delivery-layer) and [6.6](../06-implementation-guidelines.md#66-value-layer).

## 7.5 Decisions

### 7.5.1 Decides
- Which initiatives are pursued, kept, or dropped, and the roadmap.
- What is committed to Delivery, and what is promised outward.
- Changes to the lifecycle stage of features, based on evidence.
- Continuing, adjusting, stopping, or retiring initiatives and features.
- Decisions on reported debt: pay it, accept it, or defer it.
- What follows a failed validation, and whether a claim or the product is wrong when Confirm finds a contradiction.
- The answers to questions about intent.

### 7.5.2 Does not decide
- How an intent is delivered: specs, technical approach, and breakdown. These belong to the Agentic Engineer.
- Whether a result from the AI layer is accepted. This belongs to the Agentic Engineer.
- How the layers, skills, and models are defined. This belongs to the AI Enablement Lead.

## 7.6 Competencies

- **Shaping initiatives:** stating expected customer and business value, evidence of success, and a stop condition before work starts.
- **Writing intent:** what is wanted and why, without the solution.
- **Working from evidence:** weighing what customers do over what they say, and both over opinion.
- **Portfolio thinking:** giving new ideas, upkeep of mature features, and retirement each their place.
- **Willingness to stop:** ending an initiative when the evidence says so, without letting sunk cost justify continuing.
- **Directing AI as an instrument:** framing requests well, challenging drafts, and never confirming what has not been checked.
